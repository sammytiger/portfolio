Case Study · Emotion-Adaptive Interface R&D

A stress intervention that works before you know you need it.

Somatag is a screenless, body-worn protocol that detects autonomic dysregulation through HRV and intervenes with gentle haptic cues — before conscious awareness, before escalation, before the user is asked to do anything about it.

Thesis

The market optimized for monitoring. Nobody built for regulation.

Origin

A caregiver's question, asked inside a healthcare system.

This work began as a design research question, shaped by years of caregiving for my grandfather through Alzheimer's and years inside healthcare systems at CVS, Philips, and adjacent to clinical workflows. The pattern was consistent across both: the people most affected by chronic stress dysregulation — caregivers, veterans, first responders, women in perimenopause — were being handed apps that created more cognitive load, wearables that generated surveillance anxiety, and dashboards that made them feel worse.

Somatag started with a simpler question. What would a stress intervention look like if it worked the way the nervous system actually works — through the body, not around it? Through sensation, not self-reporting? Before escalation, not after?

The Gap

Why current tools fail the people who need them most.

Three failure modes, named plainly. Every Somatag design decision is a refusal of one of them.

01

Screen fatigue is the problem they keep adding screens to solve.

Notification-based interventions ask people to engage cognitively with their stress in the moment they are most cognitively overwhelmed. The tool becomes another demand.

02

Monitoring without intervention changes the score, not the outcome.

Knowing your HRV is low does not reduce it. A dashboard that shows you your stress level adds information to an already overwhelmed system.

03

Medical aesthetics create resistance in non-medical populations.

A wearable that looks like a medical device signals surveillance, stigma, and patient identity. The people most in need are least likely to adopt tools that make them feel like patients.

Jobs to Be Done

Three layers. The design had to serve all three, or it would fail at adoption.

Functional, social, emotional — synthesized from qualitative research with high-load nervous systems. Each layer is a design constraint, not a consumer insight.

Functional

Detect stress earlier than conscious awareness. Deliver an interrupt, not a readout.

The user needs a signal that is earlier and more reliable than self-report. The tool acts before the person has capacity to act.

Social

Feel like self-care, not like a medical device.

Wearable in public without generating questions. Identity-compatible. Closer to jewelry than to a heart rate monitor. This is not cosmetic — it is the adoption mechanism.

Emotional

Feel grounded and cared for — not surveilled.

The emotional register of the intervention matters as much as its clinical efficacy. A tool that makes someone feel monitored defeats its own purpose.

Design Research

Signal architecture first. Form factor second.

Rather than prototyping and testing usability, this project started at the physiological and behavioral layer — understanding the signal before designing toward it.

01

Physiological Synthesis

Mapped HRV, posture, and stillness detection to predict rumination onset. Established multi-sensor input logic to reduce false positives and identify the threshold between early dysregulation and conscious awareness.

02

Persona Simulation

Four archetypes with distinct baseline HRV curves — the burned-out caregiver, the hypervigilant veteran, the perimenopausal professional, the recovering addict. Tested intervention timing against each physiological profile.

03

Foresight Matrix

Mapped the design against calm tech principles, target user needs, and ambient computing futures. Stress-tested against Oura, Sensate, and Emotibit to define where Somatag differentiates and where it integrates.

04

Prototyping & Partnership

Form factor developed in Figma and 3D jewelry mockups with haptic zones. Manufacturing partnership secured with sensor designer. Investor pitch presented at Boulder Startup Week. Intervention algorithm ~50% complete.

Design Criteria

Three non-negotiables. Everything else is tradeable.

01

Screenless. Always.

The intervention cannot require looking at anything, opening anything, or deciding anything. It works in the body's language — haptic, subtle, preconscious — not the dashboard's language.

02

Adaptive to the individual, not to population averages.

Generic HRV thresholds produce false positives and erode trust. Baselines adapt to the individual's own physiological range over time. The system learns the person, not the norm.

03

Worn as self-expression, not as medical equipment.

Trust and aesthetics are strategic differentiators. A wearable someone wants to wear invites consent instead of creating resistance. The form factor competes with jewelry, not with heart rate monitors.

Pilot Thesis · Perimenopause Beachhead

A defined cohort. A measurable cost curve. A plausible 12-month proof.

Perimenopausal women drive ER visits (cardiac scares), sleep medication use, and mental health utilization. A 15–20% reduction in utilization for a defined cohort projects to $5–10M in annual savings per 100k covered lives against a ~$15M investment — plus Medicare Advantage differentiation on member experience.

Market tailwinds: Femtech at ~$60B with 16% CAGR. Women are 70%+ of active wearable users. Hims & Hers menopause line, IdentifyHer at CES 2025, LunaFlow AI — the category is already moving. Somatag is the regulation layer beneath it.

Current State

From a concept to an intervention engine.

Where it is today

Components validated — haptic devices (Sensate) and biosensors (Emotibit). The intervention algorithm is approximately 50% complete. Manufacturing partnership secured. Investor pitch presented at Boulder Startup Week.

The pendant is the first node in a larger system — the most intimate point of contact with the regulation protocol. Over time the somatic bundle expands: soundscapes, tactile field kits, adaptive lighting environments. Hardware and software are delivery vehicles. The protocol is the product.

Long-term positioning: a protocol licensing platform — an intervention engine that can run underneath other wearables and ambient systems, not just its own hardware. The pendant generates the behavioral dataset that gives timing precision; licensing gives scale.

Seeking

  • Research partners for clinical validation (n=10–20 feasibility study)
  • NIH Blueprint MedTech alignment
  • Academic collaborators — HRV and autonomic neuroscience
  • Integration partners across femtech, aging-in-place, and Medicare Advantage

Build the intervention layer that should already exist.

Somatag is not a wellness product. It is a regulation protocol with a pendant as its first delivery vehicle — the missing layer between wellness trackers and clinical infrastructure. Built with the people it's for, designed to disappear into a life instead of demanding one.
