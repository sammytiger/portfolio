Research

Four questions I keep returning to.

The gap between what we can build and what people want to live with isn't technical. It's neurobiological.

  1. 01

    Emotionally intelligent systems

    Interfaces that respond to human states and strengthen judgment rather than replace it. How do you personalize without surveillance? A nurse who gets burned once by a confident system stops trusting the accurate ones too.

  2. 02

    Healthcare and aging futures

    Most people intend to age at home, and payment models are pushing care the same way. The grab bar goes in after the fall. The house is the last thing anyone designs.

  3. 03

    Neurodiversity and emotional regulation

    Sensory and cognitive difference as a design input from day one, not an accommodation bolted on later. A system that assumes a steady hand and a quiet room excludes most people on most days.

  4. 04

    Adaptive technologies for daily living

    Medication, mobility, cognitive scaffolding, and the invisible work that holds a day together. The pill organizer on the counter is the real interface.

Your guide

Christine Galligan, MHA

I design systems that remember the humanity healthcare keeps forgetting. Across Clemson's Center for Success in Aging, CVS Health, Philips, Novant Health, and now SOMA Systems, two theses have held: environment regulates physiology as powerfully as medication, and participation is impossible when the system is illegible.

Research and field papers go out under Kutuhala Studio — Sanskrit, roughly, for the desire to understand. I also run Hacky Snacky, a monthly edge-computing meetup in Boulder, and I'm forming the Umwelten Institute.

Futures Worth Belonging To is the studio's series. It lives in Field Notes today; Season 1 of the podcast is in production. Photography commissions, speaking, and short notes all welcome.
