Thesis

Design that reaches seventy million patients can't start with a prototype. It starts with the system the prototype is going to live inside.

The work wasn't the design. It was getting the design through the system.

The Problem

A $300B adherence problem, hidden in plain sight.

Medication non-adherence is one of the largest under-addressed cost drivers in US healthcare — an estimated $300B annually in preventable hospitalizations, disease progression, and excess utilization. The populations most affected are exactly the ones conventional digital health tools reach least: older adults, Medicare and Medicaid members, people navigating chronic conditions across fragmented care.

Pharmacy is the highest-frequency touchpoint in American healthcare. CVS had the infrastructure, the patient relationships, and the operational scale. What it didn't yet have was a behavior change layer designed on top of that infrastructure — built on evidence, tested in the field, and able to cross legal, clinical, product, and marketing incentives without losing its soul.

The question was never whether to intervene. It was how to intervene at scale without flattening the intervention into something useless.

Approach

Multidisciplinary by design. Cross-functional by necessity.

The work fused three disciplines most healthcare product teams keep in separate rooms. The integration is the method.

Discipline 01

Research grounded in the field, not in the deck.

Qualitative research and ethnographic study inside hundreds of CVS stores. Patient journey mapping for conditions like depression, asthma, and cardiac. The insight work drove the design, not the other way around.

Discipline 02

Behavioral science translated into interaction.

Behavioral economics frameworks applied to interactive messaging, gamified pharmacy technology, and adherence nudges. Design sprints structured to move from insight to testable prototype in weeks, not quarters.

Discipline 03

Cross-functional orchestration as a core skill.

Liaison across design, product management, marketing, legal, and clinical. Most health-system pilots die in the handoff. These didn't — because the handoff was designed before the prototype was.

Process

How the pilots moved from insight to scale.

01

Qualitative Field Research

Ethnographic work in pharmacies and patient homes. Semi-structured interviews with chronic condition patients, caregivers, and pharmacy staff.

02

Patient Journey Mapping

Mapped end-to-end journeys for complex conditions — depression, asthma, cardiac — to surface the moments where behavior change is actually possible.

03

Design Sprints

Rapid design sprints to translate insight into testable prototypes. Concepts stress-tested across behavioral, clinical, and operational constraints in the same week.

04

Prototyping & Pilot

Wireframes and interactive prototypes in Adobe Sketch. AR prototyping in 8th Wall. In-store pilots in 20+ pharmacies with real patients and real staff.

05

Scale & Handoff

Measurement protocols, operational handoff to Aetna Care teams and Health Hubs, and documentation designed for programs to survive the transition from pilot to production.

Featured Work

Augmented reality for health literacy — because comprehension is a perceptual problem before it's a cognitive one.

Built an AR prototype in 8th Wall for asthma and medication adherence — deployed inside CVS's Social Determinants of Health and HealthHub portfolios. The insight: for older adults and low-literacy populations, spatial and embodied information outperforms text-heavy screens. Comprehension isn't just about the words. It's about whether the information meets the body.

Cross-Functional Leadership

Most pilots don't die from bad design. They die in translation.

Enterprise healthcare doesn't fail at ideas — it fails at the handoff between the people who had the idea and the people who have to operate it. I spent as much time aligning incentives across disciplines as I did designing the interventions themselves. That wasn't overhead. That was the work.

"The design is a 10% of the job. Getting it through legal, clinical, product, marketing, and operations — with the original intent intact — is the other 90%. The case studies you see survived because that 90% was also designed."
Design Product Marketing Legal Clinical Operations
What This Changed

Scale validated the thesis. Adoption extended it.

The work didn't end with pilot results. It graduated into operating programs, informed the health-system strategy work that followed, and reinforced the core thesis that environment + behavior + design outperform any single-discipline intervention.

  • Adoption
    Pilot programs were adopted into production by Aetna Care teams and Health Hubs.
  • Portfolio
    Substantially expanded CVS's Social Determinants of Health portfolio — closing care gaps and improving literacy across asthma, cardiac, and mental health.
  • Cost
    Contributed to medical cost savings across Medicare Advantage and chronic-condition populations.
  • Lineage
    Informed the Novant Health patient experience framework that followed — and the Somatag R&D that eventually came out of both.
