The hospital decision environment — unified, adaptive, humane.

6-month scope0→1 ConceptLead Strategist
01The Situation

Inside most hospitals, patient flow, workforce operations, and executive intelligence run as three disconnected systems — which means the people managing all three are stitching together a real-time picture by hand, under pressure, constantly.

02The Approach

A 0→1 product strategy and service-design concept for a Healthcare Control Center: one adaptive interface unifying patient flow, workforce operations, and executive intelligence, with a forward-looking AI-assisted operations copilot layered on top.

03The Outcome

Concept-stage — a six-month strategy scope defining what the product should be before anyone builds it, which is exactly where a strategist's work is supposed to end and an engineering team's begins.

04The Handoff That Matters

"Concept" undersells what six months of strategy has to produce for engineering to move: which of the three data streams gets unified first, what the interface's core states are, which decisions stay human and which get automated, and what the service looks like around the screen.

The deliverable was a build sequence with reasons — the kind of document that lets an engineering team start estimating instead of interviewing. That's where a strategist's work should end: not with a vision, but with a backlog someone can own.
